FGSM3 · DAY 3 · GROUP CHALLENGE

MSF Go-Bag Challenge

Pack the mission. Then adapt.

Your emergency team is leaving for a humanitarian response. You have limited space and incomplete information. Choose the ten items you need most, then revise the bag as the situation becomes clearer.

Team3–4 students
Device1 shared screen
Initial bagExactly 10 items
Final output2-minute team briefing

How the challenge works

You always see the same information. There are no secret roles. Read each update together, discuss before clicking, and keep exactly ten items in the bag until the final emergency request.

1 · PACKChoose exactly 10 items.
2 · ADAPTFlooding update: replace up to 3.
3 · ADAPT AGAINMedical update: replace up to 2.
4 · FINAL CHANGEAccess update: replace up to 1.
5 · PRIORITISEClassify your 10 items as 3 / 4 / 3.
6 · EMERGENCY REQUESTAdd one final item.
7 · BRIEFPrepare one coherent team answer.
8 · EVERYONE SPEAKSEach student contributes orally.
Speaking rule: before every Continue button, every student must give at least one reason, question or objection. Tick the confirmation box only after everyone has spoken.
ROUND 1 · THE EMPTY BAG

Choose your first 10 items

You do not yet know exactly what the emergency is. Your team can take only ten items. There is no single perfect answer: your job is to build a defensible first-response kit.

Your task

Select exactly 10 items. Discuss what each item could help you do. Do not click Continue until everyone has contributed.

0 / 10 selected
Emergency equipment bankClick an item to add or remove it.
Talk before you click Continue

Which needs are you trying to cover? What are you choosing not to take, and why?

ROUND 2 · FIELD UPDATE

Severe flooding

Now you know where the team is going. Review the information carefully, then decide whether your original bag still makes sense.

  • Severe flooding has affected a remote region.
  • Around 8,000 people have been displaced.
  • Many families are living in temporary shelters.
  • The local health centre has been damaged.
Your task

Keep exactly 10 items. You may replace up to 3 items from your first bag. To replace an item, remove one selected item first, then add one unselected item. You may also keep the bag unchanged.

10 / 10 selected0 / 3 replacements
Questions to discuss

Does flooding make water, shelter or mobility more urgent? Which of your original assumptions have changed?

ROUND 3 · MEDICAL UPDATE

New patient needs

The first clinical reports have reached the team. You have one more chance to adapt the kit.

  • Many patients have diarrhoea and vomiting.
  • Dehydration is becoming a major concern.
  • Several patients have infected wounds.
  • Several pregnant women also need medical care.
Your task

Keep exactly 10 items. Compared with your flooding bag, you may replace up to 2 items. Remove a selected item first, then add its replacement.

10 / 10 selected0 / 2 replacements
Questions to discuss

Which items now have a clear clinical purpose? Is there anything useful that is still less urgent than hydration, wound care or maternal care?

ROUND 4 · LOGISTICS UPDATE

Access is getting harder

The response may have to operate more independently than expected. Make your final change carefully.

  • The main road has been destroyed.
  • New supplies may not arrive for 48 hours.
  • Electricity is unreliable.
  • Clean drinking water is becoming scarce.
Your task

Keep exactly 10 items. Compared with the medical-response bag, you may replace only 1 item. Remove one selected item first, then add its replacement.

10 / 10 selected0 / 1 replacement
Use a conditional while you discuss

Try: If supplies are delayed, we will… / If clean water becomes unavailable, we will… / Even if access becomes difficult, we will…

ROUND 5 · PRIORITY BOARD

Not all 10 items are equally important

Your bag is now locked. Classify every item so your team can explain what it would protect first.

Your task

Classify the 10 items as 3 Essential, 4 Very important and 3 Useful. Every item must have exactly one label.

Talk before you continue

Which three items would be hardest to work without? Which useful item did not make your top seven?

ROUND 6 · FINAL UPDATE

One more request

A second group of 2,000 displaced people has arrived. Headquarters can send one additional item from the equipment you left behind.

Your task

Choose one additional item. It does not replace anything in your bag: it will be added as your emergency request.

0 / 1 requested
Explain the change

Why has this item become more important now that 2,000 additional displaced people have arrived?

FINAL · MSF TEAM BRIEFING

Your mission kit is ready

Use the report below as evidence. Do not read it word for word: turn it into one connected two-minute team briefing.

🎙️ Your 2-minute team briefing

Every student must speak. Share these five points across the group:

  1. Summarise the humanitarian situation.
  2. Name your three Essential items and explain why they matter.
  3. Describe one difficult choice your team made.
  4. Explain your one emergency request.
  5. Finish with a contingency sentence: If… we will… or Even if… we will…
PrioritisingOur immediate priority is…
We prioritised X over Y because…
Trade-offsWe decided not to take… because…
The difficult choice was between…
ContingencyIf supplies are delayed, we will…
Even if access becomes difficult, we will…
