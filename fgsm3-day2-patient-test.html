MISSION BRIEF

How the Patient Test works

1 · BUILD

Make seven policy decisions with a limited budget. Every choice creates a trade-off.

2 · TEST

Test seven fictional patient profiles. Your own policies determine whether each person is protected, at risk or failed.

3 · SURVIVE

A national crisis hits your system. Choose one response using whatever resources you kept.

4 · REFORM + DEFEND

Choose exactly two reforms, run the Patient Test again, then prepare a shared Commission Hearing.

Rule: before each policy vote, patient-outcome reveal, crisis response or reform choice, at least two students must speak. Do not click until two voices have been heard. This is a speaking task disguised as a strategy game.
⏱
FINAL SPEAKING RULE · Every student speaks for about 2 minutes. A 3-person team should aim for roughly 6 minutes; a 4-person team for roughly 8 minutes.
YOUR FINAL OUTPUT

What your group must present

3 · Crisis + reformsExplain your crisis response, your two reforms and what changed after the second Patient Test.
4 · Final evaluationState one strength, one remaining weakness and the main trade-off your commission accepts.

PHASE 1 · NATIONAL HEALTH COMMISSION

Take your seats

Choose 3 or 4 students. Then each student reveals one role card and reads the role priority aloud. With 3 students, the Clinical Adviser also covers workforce. The Build phase stays locked until every assigned role has been revealed at least once.

How many students?

Four separate roles will be assigned.

Commission rule

For every policy decision, the Finance Minister and the Equity Officer must both speak before the team votes.

For patient cases, the Clinical Adviser leads the discussion.

Reveal every role to unlock the Build phase.

PHASE 2 · BUILD YOUR SYSTEM

Seven decisions. One budget.

You have 21 Health Credits. Expensive choices often improve access or equity; cheaper choices may protect the budget or speed up care for some patients. There is no perfect combination.

Before clicking: Finance Minister — can we afford it? Equity Officer — who might lose out? Clinical Adviser — what could happen to patients? Workforce Representative — can staff deliver it?

🎙️ FINAL COMMISSION HEARING · 2 MINUTES PER STUDENT

Defend your healthcare system as one team

🗣
Every student must speak for about 2 minutes. Aim for a connected 6–8 minute group presentation, not separate unrelated mini-talks.
Speaker focus A · BuildHow is the system funded and organised? Which two major design choices best define it, and why?
Speaker focus B · PatientsWhich patient cases exposed the biggest access, equity, waiting-time or workforce problem?
Speaker focus C · Crisis + reformWhat crisis hit the system? Why did you choose your response and your two reforms? What improved?
Speaker focus D · VerdictWhat is the system's strongest dimension? What weakness remains? What trade-off would you defend to the commission?

For a 3-person team: combine Speaker focuses C and D. For a 4-person team: one student can lead each focus. Everyone should refer to the same system dossier and build on the previous speaker.

  1. Use at least one concrete patient example.
  2. Refer to one before/after reform change.
  3. Use at least one comparison or concession: whereas, compared with, although, however, on balance.
  4. Finish with one clear recommendation or defended trade-off.
Our system is mainly funded by…Compared with our original model…One patient who exposed a weakness was…Although this reform improved…, it also…On balance, we would defend… because…
